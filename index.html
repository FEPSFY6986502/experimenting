01. Abstract

Executive Summary: Automated decision-making (ADM) systems are increasingly implemented in retail consumer credit evaluations to streamline risk modeling. However, unweighted proxy variables frequently perpetuate legacy historical disparities. This paper evaluates the operational efficiency of fairness-constrained re-weighting algorithms on binary credit classification datasets (n=12,450).

Our quantitative testing indicates that applying pre-processing equalized odds constraints reduced error rate discrepancies across demographic cohorts by 27.8 percentage points, accompanied by a minimal accuracy trade-off of 1.4%.

02. Introduction

The transition from conventional human-underwritten underwriting to machine learning scoring models has accelerated financial institution processing times globally. Despite these operational efficiencies, machine learning algorithms model future predictions on historical datasets that inherently reflect prior socio-economic disparities (Barocas and Selbst, 2016).

Primary Research Question
"To what degree can mathematical re-weighting constraints eliminate demographic parity variance in credit models without destabilizing primary predictive accuracy?"

03. Methodology

An anonymized financial dataset of 12,450 loan applications was evaluated using a Logistic Regression classifier and a Random Forest pipeline in Python.

Data Pre-processing

Missing attributes were imputed via median estimators. Features were scaled using standard z-score normalization.

Fairness Metrics

Demographic Parity and Equalized Odds were computed across protected groups comparing false positive rates.

04. Empirical Results

Applying algorithmic re-weighting constraints drastically reduced error variance across cohorts while retaining overall performance metrics.

05. Discussion

The results demonstrate that algorithmic fairness does not require a significant compromise in predictive utility. The minor 1.4% drop in overall accuracy is heavily offset by reducing error disparity from 27.8% down to 0.9%.

06. Conclusion

Proactive algorithmic auditing must become a standard operational protocol in data engineering pipelines. Integrating pre-processing constraints eliminates systematic bias towards protected demographics.
